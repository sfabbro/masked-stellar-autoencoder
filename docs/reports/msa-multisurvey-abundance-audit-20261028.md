## Transformations and joint observation models

- Gaia's documented coordinate `[Fe I/H] = [M/H] + [Fe I/M]` is an algebraic conversion only when the same source/reference conventions apply. Both native measurements and provenance must remain available; a current helper `src/masked_stellar_autoencoder/training/multisurvey_abundances.py` accepts an explicit reference offset and propagates a supplied 2x2 covariance. Gaia's marginal percentiles do not supply their joint covariance. If covariance is absent, the converted variance remains unknown rather than assuming zero correlation. Do not score the derived Fe/H and its constituent M/H and Fe/M as three independent labels: they are deterministic/shared-spectrum dependent quantities. Even a correct conversion does not calibrate Gaia's scale to APOGEE, GALAH or DESI.
- A formal bracket-identity `[alpha/Fe] = [alpha/M] + [M/H] - [Fe/H]` holds only for the *same explicitly defined abundance and reference system*. APOGEE's atmospheric `ALPHA_M` is a spectral-template parameter; DESI RVSpecFit `ALPHAFE` and a GALAH combination of several `X_fe` are not necessarily the same measured aggregate. An arithmetic reference change alone cannot establish interchangeability. If any derived combination is retained for diagnostics, preserve its ingredients, conventions, covariance uncertainty, and the fact that it is dependent on the inputs.
- Best near-term modeling is one latent vector per star plus sparse source-specific observation functions `y_s = h_s(theta, quality) + epsilon_s`, with per-record masks, units, explicit abundance definition, native uncertainties or declared unknown-error policy, survey/cross-spectrum dependencies and a versioned release. Map directly comparable X/Fe element quantities first; model global M/H or alpha/M through their own observation maps rather than relabeling them as Fe/H or a universal alpha. Prioritize Mg and Si as common element anchors, while keeping element sensitivity/domain caveats and upper-limit flags. APOGEE documents named `FE_H`, `MG_FE`/other X/Fe tags and their `*_ERR`/`*_FLAG`; its per-element/atmospheric covariance is not provided (https://www.sdss4.org/dr17/irspec/abundances/). GALAH individual elements similarly need their own flags and common-element overlap.
- Fit relative survey offsets/scales on overlapping **FIT** stars with smoothness/shrinkage and a fixed reference or gauge; validate in held-out stars and by Teff/logg/metallicity, S/N, and sky/field. A connected overlap graph identifies relative scales only in supported regions and up to the chosen gauge. Anchors are needed to claim a physical common scale; annotate disconnected or extrapolated regions. Use source-specific robust/noise terms and avoid pretending the label-error covariances are known. Do not optimize a mapping using the held-out benchmark it will be judged on.
- `/private/tmp/torchregress-multisurvey-20261026/src/torchregress/calibration/multisource.py:176` provides a configurable-D `RelativeSurveySplineHead` with an exact fixed-weight per-target relative gauge and Jacobian. The current MSA `pack_multisurvey_observations` in `src/masked_stellar_autoencoder/training/multisurvey_model.py:114-151` explicitly rejects D != 3 and assumes identity-compatible Teff/logg/Fe/H; the sidecar `multisurvey_overlay.py` is likewise fixed to three targets. `alpha_heads.py` is disabled/interface-only and is not evidence that a multi-survey alpha mapping has been trained. Therefore the durable missing work is an expanded sparse measurement schema + mapped likelihood, per-source alpha/element ingestion, correlated six-target head and actual validation, not merely changing a column alias.
