# Age-dating red giants (RGB, red clump, AGB): asteroseismology, spectroscopic/photometric mass proxies, and data-driven age transfer (status as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: giant ages are almost entirely **mass ages**. On the RGB, age ≈ main-sequence lifetime of the progenitor, roughly t ∝ M^-3 or steeper, so a fractional mass error σ_M/M becomes about 3–4× larger in fractional age. Every method below estimates mass (or Δν/νmax, which give mass) and converts it with stellar models. Sources are arXiv abstracts or full texts read in this session, 18 Nov–22 Nov 2026. Results before 2020 are marked **[older]**.

---

## 1. Asteroseismic ages: scaling relations, corrections, grid modelling, mass loss, catalogue generations, accuracy tests, metal-poor/α-rich problems

### Takeaway
On the lower RGB and red clump, seismic masses calibrated to Gaia radii (APOKASC-3) reach about 4% in mass and about 11% in age (median, best 10k stars). Model-to-model age systematics are about 10%, and the absolute systematic on the thick-disc age is about ±0.9 Gyr. Accuracy gets clearly worse for luminous giants (RGB tip, AGB), for red-clump stars (mass loss of about 0.1 M⊙ must be modelled), and for metal-poor or α-rich stars. There, uncorrected scaling relations give masses that are too high, or too low (ages older than the Universe), depending on the Teff scale, the νmax relation and the Δν correction. Cluster tests still show tension beyond the quoted errors (NGC 6819, M4), and eclipsing binaries show that uncorrected scaling relations overestimate red-giant mass by about 12–26%.

### Cited Findings

**Scaling relations and corrections**
- APOKASC-3 writes the relations as M/M⊙ = (f_νmax νmax/νmax,⊙)^3 (f_Δν Δν/Δν⊙)^-4 (Teff/Teff,⊙)^1.5 and R/R⊙ = (f_νmax νmax/νmax,⊙)(f_Δν Δν/Δν⊙)^-2 (Teff/Teff,⊙)^0.5. f_Δν comes from stellar-interior models. f_νmax is an empirical function of νmax calibrated to fundamental (Gaia) radii. In APOKASC-2, f_νmax = 1 with νmax,⊙ = 3076 µHz. — [Pinsonneault et al. 2025, APOKASC-3 (arXiv:2410.00102)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- Sharma et al. 2016 **[older]** compared about 13,000 Kepler red giants with the Galaxia population-synthesis model. The model overpredicts low-mass (old, metal-poor) stars, and applying model-based Δν corrections (released as the asfgrid code) "significantly reduces" the discrepancy. — [Sharma et al. 2016 (arXiv:1603.05661)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05661)
- T. Li et al. 2022 fitted radial-mode frequencies of 3,642 Kepler RGB stars. Typical uncertainties are 4.5% in mass, 16% in age, 0.006 dex in log g and 1.7% in radius. The scaling relations show a systematic offset of about 15% in mass and about 7% in radius relative to the modelling solutions, mainly because the uncorrected Δν scaling underestimates Δν by about 4% on average (the surface term matters). There is no significant νmax offset, but there is a clear metallicity dependence. — [T. Li et al. 2022 (arXiv:2201.09577)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09577)
- Cao & Pinsonneault 2025 propose an alternative correction scheme to APOKASC-3's. For the α-rich population it reduces the lower-RGB vs upper-RGB median mass discrepancy from 6.65% to 1.72% and the age discrepancy from −21.81% to −9.55%. It also reduces sensitivity to the method used to turn frequency spacings into mean density. — [Cao & Pinsonneault 2025 (arXiv:2508.06609)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06609)
- With APOKASC-3 masses, the observed first-dredge-up change in [C/N] is smaller than MESA predicts by 0.16 ± 0.08 (obs) ± 0.01 (sys) dex. The observed RGB bump is 0.15 dex higher in log g than predicted. The empirical bump locus is log g = 2.6604 − 0.1832(M/M⊙ − 1) + 0.2824[Fe/H]. — [Cao & Pinsonneault 2025, FDU/RGBB (arXiv:2501.14867)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14867)

**APOKASC-2 → APOKASC-3**
- APOKASC-2 **[older, 2018]** had 6,676 evolved stars and 5 seismic pipelines. It used theory-based f_Δν and calibrated the νmax zero point to masses of NGC 6791/NGC 6819 members. — [Pinsonneault et al. 2018 (arXiv:1804.09983)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09983)
- APOKASC-3 covers the complete Kepler×APOGEE evolved sample of 15,808 stars and uses 10 seismic pipelines. It gives evolutionary state, mass, radius and age for 12,418 stars. Of these, 10,036 are "exceptionally precise", with median fractional uncertainties of 0.6% (νmax), 0.6% (Δν), 3.8% (mass), 1.8% (radius) and **11.1% (age)**. A further 1,624 stars have more limited data. The fundamental scale is anchored on radii from Gaia L plus APOGEE Teff, rather than on cluster masses. — [Pinsonneault et al. 2025 (arXiv:2410.00102)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102) (a search summary quoted 12,448; the arXiv abstract says 12,418)
- APOKASC-3 ages use GARSTEC models; lower-RGB and RC stars are fitted only with models in the appropriate evolutionary state. A comparison with alternative YREC ages (no overshoot, no settling) shows trends at the **±10% level for most RGB stars** and larger differences for more massive RC stars. At low mass the main systematics are gravitational settling and mass loss; at high mass they are core overshoot. — [APOKASC-3 full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- From lower-RGB stars, the median age of the chemical thick disc is **9.14 ± 0.05 (ran) ± 0.9 (sys) Gyr**, with an age dispersion of 1.1 Gyr that is consistent with the error model. The sample also shows a sharp upper age boundary in the thin disc. — [APOKASC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- The scaling relations are "precise and accurate on the lower RGB and RC", but become more model-dependent for luminous giants and "break down at the tip of the RGB". Before calibration, low-νmax RGB radii disagree with Gaia radii by up to about 10%, and the offset depends on the f_Δν method. For moderate- and high-luminosity α-rich stars, ages differ from lower-RGB ages by **17–37%** (mass offsets of 6–12%). — [APOKASC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- Domain caveats: for [Fe/H] < −1 the IRFM temperature scale has little data, so Teff may be systematically offset. νmax scaling is not corrected for metallicity, and the catalogue cautions that systematics are larger at R > 30 R⊙ and at low metallicity. — [APOKASC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)

**Mass loss and red-clump/AGB ages**
- APOKASC-3 uses the α-rich (roughly coeval) population as a pseudo-cluster. The RC–RGB mass difference gives an integrated RGB mass loss of **0.10 M⊙**, i.e. an effective Reimers **η = 0.17**. The larger RC dispersion implies a dispersion of 0.03 M⊙ in mass loss (Δη = 0.05), which is propagated into larger RC age errors. About 1/6 of luminous giants are AGB stars, and two age sets (RGB vs AGB) are given for shell-burning stars above the RC that cannot be classified. — [APOKASC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- In M4, 75 K2 red giants give an integrated RGB mass loss of 0.17 ± 0.01 M⊙ (η_R = 0.39). — [Howell et al. 2022 (arXiv:2207.02406)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.02406)
- NGC 5897 is very metal-poor and was observed by K2. Its 20 RGB stars average 0.74 ± 0.01 M⊙ and its 6 early-AGB stars 0.65 ± 0.03 M⊙, so ΔM(RGB→EAGB) = 0.08 ± 0.03 M⊙. This supports integrated RGB mass loss that **decreases with decreasing metallicity** in Type I globular clusters. — [Kalup et al. 2026 (arXiv:2609.01119)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01119)
- Field giants with the same [C/N] (a birth-mass proxy unaffected by mass loss) but different evolutionary states show net RGB mass loss that **decreases with increasing metallicity and with mass**. This contradicts a constant Reimers η, and the authors propose a mass- and metallicity-dependent η. 207 stars (3.33%) are outliers and are flagged as mass-transfer candidates. — [Roberts et al. 2026 (arXiv:2601.10800)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10800)
- Brogaard et al. 2024 derive integrated RGB mass loss and its metallicity dependence from the α-rich field sequence (Kepler/K2 + APOGEE DR17 + Gaia DR3) in three metallicity bins, and compare it with metal-rich globular clusters. The numbers were not given in the truncated abstract. — [Brogaard et al. 2024 (arXiv:2410.08330)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08330)
- In Willett et al. 2026, low-mass stars (M_scaling ≲ 0.7 M⊙) hit the 20 Gyr age-prior ceiling and are flagged. Most such stars are core-He-burning stars that lost significant mass on the upper RGB. Without a mass-loss correction their masses would imply "ages much greater than the age of the Universe", so they are removed. — [Willett et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.06870)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06870)
- A theory study **[older]** found that a wrong Reimers η biases RGB grid ages very little except above the RGB bump. In the last 2.5% of RGB life the mass error reaches 6.5%, larger than the random component. The random error alone is about 8% in mass and 30% in age for global-seismic grid fits. — [Valle et al. 2017 (arXiv:1711.03753)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03753)

**Grid-based modelling and model dependence**
- Willett et al. 2026 use **PARAM** to infer homogeneous masses, radii, ages, distances and orbits for **>17,000 red giants** from Kepler, K2 and TESS, combined with Gaia DR3 and APOGEE DR17/GALAH DR3. They flag stars with unreliable Δν by comparing with Gaia luminosities; this matters most for K2 because of its short campaigns. A 5% bias in Δν produces a strong mass/radius bias. Switching the Gaia parallax zero point from a constant 17 µas to Lindegren et al. (2021) changes luminosity-based masses by more than 20% for distant stars. The quality cut removes about 11% of K2 stars versus 0.8% (66 stars) of Kepler and 5% (72 stars) of TESS. The paper also extends mass–[C/N] relations to lower masses. — [Willett et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.06870)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06870) (reported as published in MNRAS 2026 by a citing paper; not independently verified)
- Comparing four widely used evolutionary grids on the RGB gives age differences that "occasionally exceed 80%" when mass is not known, and significant disagreements at non-solar metallicity. The authors propose propagating theoretical track uncertainties. — [Morales, Tayar & Claytor 2025 (arXiv:2504.17600)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17600)
- Using individual-mode frequencies (BASTA) instead of global parameters improves precision for very metal-poor giants. Two Kepler stars at [Fe/H] ≈ −2.5 (KIC 4671239 and KIC 7693833) were characterised this way. — [Larsen et al. 2025 (arXiv:2503.23063)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23063)

**Other seismic catalogues (APO-K2, K2 GAP, TESS)**
- K2 GAP DR3 provides radius and mass coefficients for about 19,000 red giants (C1–C8, C10–C18), calibrated to the Gaia DR2 parallax scale. Combined with GALAH DR3, ages have 20–30% precision. — [Zinn et al. 2022 (arXiv:2108.05455)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05455). The selection function is documented in [Sharma et al. 2022 (arXiv:2109.12173)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12173).
- APO-K2 I gives masses and radii for 7,673 giants (APOGEE DR17 + K2 GAP). Of these, about 288 have [M/H] ≤ −1, and their seismic masses are **larger** than astrophysical expectations. The authors attribute this to the metal-poor Teff scale rather than to seismology. — [Schonhut-Stasik et al. 2024 (arXiv:2304.10654)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10654)
- APO-K2 II gives ages for 4,661 RGB stars across 17 of 19 K2 fields, agreeing with APOKASC-3 ages to about 3%. Ignoring α-enhancement in opacities biases ages (the abstract is truncated before the magnitude). — [Warfield et al. 2024 (arXiv:2403.16250)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16250)
- The TESS QLP all-sky search found 158,505 oscillating red giants (νmax plus Gaia/colour radii). — [Hon et al. 2021 (arXiv:2108.01241)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01241)
- TESS ages for about 132,794 red giants come from custom MESA models, with an average uncertainty of 23%. The seismic measurements were validated to 5% for up to 90% of stars. — [Theodoridis, Morales & Tayar 2025 (arXiv:2505.08109)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08109)
- In the TESS CVZ (sectors 1–87), 19,151 red giants have νmax and Δν precisions of 1.5% and 1%. For the 10,298 with spectroscopy, mass and radius precisions are 7.5% and 2.8%, "comparable to 4-yr Kepler". Seismic radii agree with Gaia radii. — [Sreenivas et al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.00498)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00498)

**Accuracy tests: eclipsing binaries, Gaia radii, clusters**
- Eclipsing binaries **[older]**: uncorrected scaling relations overestimate the masses of three red giants at [Fe/H] ≈ −0.3 by 11.7%, 13.7% and 18.9%. With Rodrigues et al. (2017) f_Δν the masses agree. The binary ages are 5.3 ± 0.5, 3.1 ± 0.6 and 4.8 ± 0.5 Gyr. — [Brogaard et al. 2018 (arXiv:1801.08167)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08167)
- For KIC 8430105, uncorrected scaling relations overestimate mass by 26%, radius by 11% and mean density by 7%. Theoretical f_Δν largely reconciles them. — [Thomsen et al. 2022/23 (arXiv:2210.05410)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05410)
- The original RV + eclipse test of 10 Kepler red giants is reported in [Gaulme et al. 2016 (arXiv:1609.06645)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06645); its quantitative result was truncated in the abstract retrieved here. 2026 additions: two lower-RGB benchmarks (KIC 8129189, KIC 10920813), which the authors say confirm that the scaling relations "systematically overestimate red-giant masses and radii", with fitted f_Δν and f_νmax — [Hedlund, Gaulme et al. 2026 (arXiv:2609.17737)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17737). Also coeval interior models for 8 of 11 eclipsing binaries; using the EB giants as reference instead of the Sun brings scaling masses into agreement without extra corrections — [Yıldız et al. 2026 (arXiv:2608.18250)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18250).
- Gaia DR2 radius test **[older]**: about 3,600 Kepler first-ascent giants are on the parallax scale at +1.7% ± 0.3% (rand) ± 2.0% (syst), and dwarfs/subgiants at −2.1%. This implies a **relative dwarf–giant scaling-radius error of 4% ± 0.6%**, and departures appear above 30 R⊙. — [Zinn et al. 2019 (arXiv:1910.00719)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00719)
- Surface-brightness–colour radii versus APO-K2 seismic radii for more than 6,400 stars differ by +1.2% and −2.5% (two SBCRs). SBCR radii are lower for R > 20 R⊙. — [Valle et al. 2024 (arXiv:2409.10050)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10050)
- Cluster masses in APOKASC-3: NGC 6791 lower-RGB and RC masses are 1.15 ± 0.01 and 1.12 ± 0.01 M⊙, against a fundamental 1.15 ± 0.02 M⊙. NGC 6819 lower-RGB and RC masses are 1.65 ± 0.02 and 1.64 ± 0.02 M⊙, about 2σ above the APOKASC-2 calibration value of 1.55 ± 0.04 M⊙ and close to Handberg et al. 2017 (1.61, 1.64). — [APOKASC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- M67 (K2) **[older]**: the average red-giant mass is 1.36 ± 0.01 M⊙, consistent with the eclipsing-binary dynamical mass. Individual ages are compatible with isochrones, with no evidence of strong RGB mass loss. — [Stello et al. 2016 (arXiv:1610.03060)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03060)
- **Meta-analysis tension.** Seismic ages of individual RGB stars in NGC 6819, NGC 6791, M67, M4, M19, M80 and M9 scatter far more than the isochrone absolute-age uncertainty (5.4% for M92; at least 6% adopted) and model-to-model isochrone systematics (about 10%). Median offsets agree for M67 (cluster age 3.95 Gyr) and NGC 6791 (8.3 Gyr). They significantly exceed the uncertainties for NGC 6819 (adopted 2.54 Gyr) and M4 (11.5 Gyr). Adopted globular-cluster ages are 12–13 Gyr for M19, M80 and M9. Two NGC 1817 giants were offset by about 200% and excluded. Proposed explanations are binary mass alteration, or corrections depending on mass, metallicity or log g. — [Tayar & Joyce 2025 (arXiv:2502.09582)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09582)

**Metal-poor and α-rich giants**
- Epstein et al. 2014 **[older]**: standard scaling relations give masses **too high** for nine metal-poor APOKASC giants compared with halo/thick-disc expectations. — [Epstein et al. 2014 (arXiv:1403.1872)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.1872). APO-K2 sees the same effect at [M/H] ≤ −1 (above).
- **"Too young" from global seismology.** Individual-frequency modelling of two Helmi-stream giants gives τ = 11.16 ± 0.91 and 12.52 ± 1.05 Gyr. Ages of metal-poor, α-enhanced red giants "can be severely underestimated" when inferred from global seismic parameters. — [Lindsay et al. 2025 (arXiv:2507.01091)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01091)
- For eight evolved metal-poor α-enhanced stars, full α-enhanced opacities and ad hoc [M/H] corrections give consistent properties. The observed νmax is **larger** than the νmax scaling predicts, which argues for a metallicity-dependent νmax relation. — [Lindsay, Ong & Basu 2026 (arXiv:2603.03450)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03450)
- The metal-poor seismic binary KIC 2971380 "hints for a need to correct the νmax scaling relation, supporting recent studies of extremely metal-poor oscillators". Joint modelling of a binary in which both stars oscillate gives initial He to 3%. — [Thomsen et al. 2026, DUETS (arXiv:2610.36209)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36209)
- Too-old ages caused by mass loss: six APOKASC-2 "RGB" stars give model ages "significantly older than the age of the Galaxy" if treated as first-ascent stars. They are more likely post-mass-loss stars or RC stars, with 0.1–0.3 M⊙ lost and maximum ages of 9–9.5 Gyr. — [Örtel & Yıldız 2025 (arXiv:2510.02931)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02931)
- Montalbán et al. 2021 **[older]**: about 11% seismic ages for Kepler giants, including Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus members, used to date early Milky Way assembly. — [Montalbán et al. 2021 (arXiv:2006.01783)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01783). Miglio et al. 2021: about 5,400 Kepler+APOGEE giants, including about 400 α-rich RGB stars and an old (8–9 Gyr) super-solar low-α population — [arXiv:2004.14806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14806).
- Mass-transfer contamination: in 8,803 APOKASC-3 × MWM DR19 giants, a kNN on [Mg/H] and [Fe/Mg] predicts seismic ages to about 2 Gyr for most stars. 377 outliers are mass-accretor or mass-donor candidates, implying "a fundamental limit on the reliability of asteroseismic ages". — [Frazer et al. 2025 (arXiv:2510.26927)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26927)

### Inferences
- A realistic total age error for a lower-RGB or RC giant with Kepler-quality data is about 11% random plus about 10% model systematics, i.e. roughly 15% in quadrature. RC stars carry extra error from the mass-loss dispersion (0.03 M⊙). K2 and TESS samples are at 20–30%.
- The metal-poor regime has competing biases: Teff-scale errors push masses up (APO-K2, Epstein 2014); νmax-scaling errors and missing individual-frequency information push ages down (Lindsay 2025/2026); mass loss pushes RC/AGB ages up if ignored (Willett 2026). The sign of the net bias is not universal. Any data-driven model trained on these labels inherits a metallicity-dependent, sign-ambiguous bias at [Fe/H] < −1.
- The 2018→2025 change in anchoring (cluster masses → Gaia radii) moved the NGC 6819 mass by about 6%, roughly an 18–20% age shift at fixed models. Training labels from APOKASC-2 and APOKASC-3 are therefore not interchangeable.
- Integrated RGB mass loss trends with metallicity disagree: field stars show ΔM decreasing with increasing [Fe/H] (Roberts 2026; MacDonald & Mullan 2025 summary, [arXiv:2508.05480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05480)), while globular clusters show ΔM decreasing with decreasing [Fe/H] (Kalup 2026). This is a live systematic for RC ages of metal-poor and old stars.

### Gaps
- No single source here gave separate fractional age uncertainties for lower RGB vs RC vs upper RGB in APOKASC-3. Only the overall 11.1% median and the qualitative statement that RC uncertainty is enhanced were retrieved.
- The quantitative results of Gaulme et al. 2016 (mass/radius overestimates) and Brogaard et al. 2024 (mass loss vs [Fe/H]) were truncated in the retrieved abstracts.
- Yaguang Li et al. papers (Gaia-based Δν corrections, post-mass-transfer He-burning giants, 2022–2025) are referenced by Willett 2026 but were not read directly.
- No source here quantified the fraction of APOKASC-3 or APO-K2 metal-poor stars with ages above 13.8 Gyr.

## 2. Spectroscopic (and photometric) mass/age proxies: [C/N], Li, other abundances; calibrations and validity ranges; can Gaia XP or broadband photometry constrain [C/N] or mass?

### Takeaway
After the first dredge-up, [C/N] gives mass to about 14% (0.2 M⊙) and age to about 40% for disc giants (Martig 2016, Ness 2016). The 2025 APOKASC-3-calibrated relation recovers ages of 1–10 Gyr with a mean uncertainty of about 1.6 Gyr, and cluster calibrations (Casali 2019, Spoo 2022) have now been extended to metal-poor globular clusters (Spoo 2025). Validity is limited by extra mixing above the RGB bump, the birth [C/N] trend, abundance-pipeline zero points, and mass transfer. Gaia XP does carry information that predicts Δν, νmax and ΔΠ1 and mass/age (via machine learning), but I found no direct XP [C/N] measurement.

### Cited Findings
- Martig et al. 2016 **[older]**: a calibration on 1,475 Kepler+APOGEE DR12 giants predicts mass from [C/H], [N/H], log g, Teff and [Fe/H] with about 14% rms (typically 0.2 M⊙), giving ages with about 40% rms over 1–13 Gyr. It was applied to 52,000 DR12 giants. — [Martig et al. 2016 (arXiv:1511.08203)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08203)
- Ness et al. 2016 **[older]**: The Cannon gives mass to about 0.07 dex from APOGEE DR12 spectra, implying ages to about 0.2 dex (40%). The information comes mainly from CN regions (mass-dependent dredge-up). The catalogue has about 80,000 giants. — [Ness et al. 2016 (arXiv:1511.08204)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08204)
- Casali et al. 2019 **[older]**: an empirical age–[C/N] relation calibrated on open clusters observed by both Gaia-ESO and APOGEE. Isochrones define, for each age and metallicity, the log g window between the first dredge-up and the RGB bump. — [Casali et al. 2019 (arXiv:1907.07350)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07350)
- Spoo et al. 2022 (OCCAM VII): a [C/N]–age calibration for APOGEE DR17 evolved stars, primarily RGB, from open clusters. It explores how red-clump and other mixing affects the relation. — [Spoo et al. 2022 (arXiv:2203.05463)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05463)
- Spoo et al. 2025 extend the [C/N]–age calibration to older, metal-poor stars by adding 47 Tuc, M71, M4 and M5 to the open-cluster sample. It applies to APOGEE DR17 giants after the first dredge-up and **before extra mixing**. — [Spoo et al. 2025 (arXiv:2510.17579)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17579)
- Roberts et al. 2025 calibrate [C/N]–age on APOGEE DR17 + APOKASC-3. It "reliably recovers asteroseismic ages between 1 and 10 Gyr with average uncertainties of 1.64 Gyr" and gives concordant ages, with modest offsets, across evolutionary states. The [C/N]–birth-mass relation is robust for **luminous giants**, which is an advantage over direct seismology there. A minimum log-age uncertainty of 0.05 dex is assumed. — [Roberts et al. 2025 (arXiv:2509.25321)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25321)
- [C/N] tracks birth mass and is not reset by mass loss, so comparing [C/N] masses with seismic masses diagnoses mass loss and mass transfer. 3.33% of stars are outliers (likely mass-transfer products). — [Roberts et al. 2026 (arXiv:2601.10800)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10800)
- Theory vs data: the observed first-dredge-up Δ[C/N] is about 0.16 dex smaller than MESA predicts, which is consistent with abundance systematics. Envelope undershooting, proposed to fix the RGB bump, would increase Li destruction in the first dredge-up at high [Fe/H], in tension with GALAH Li. **Li in the first dredge-up is proposed as a sensitive test.** — [Cao & Pinsonneault 2025 (arXiv:2501.14867)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14867)
- Willett et al. 2026 extend known mass–[C/N] relations to lower masses using K2 and TESS together with Kepler. — [Willett et al. 2026 (arXiv:2602.06870)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06870)
- In APO-K2 RGB stars, [(C+N+O)/Fe] rises more steeply with [α/Fe] than uniform α-enhancement predicts ([O/Fe] about 0.1 dex higher at [α/Fe] = 0.3). The impact on evolutionary timescales is "rather limited". — [Valle et al. 2024 (arXiv:2407.05960)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05960)
- In M67, the APOGEE ASPCAP (DR12) [C/N] of subgiants is overestimated by about 0.2 dex because of a Teff/log g offset. This illustrates pipeline zero-point sensitivity. — [Bertelli Motta et al. 2017 [older] (arXiv:1701.00979)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.00979)
- Other chemical clocks: [Mg/Ce] + [C/N] + kinematics in a CatBoost model (see Q3). [Mg/H] + [Fe/Mg] predict seismic ages to about 2 Gyr by kNN for MWM DR19 giants. — [Boulet 2024 (arXiv:2403.20291)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20291); [Frazer et al. 2025 (arXiv:2510.26927)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26927)
- **Gaia XP → seismology.** CNN-LSTM models trained on Kepler giants recover Δν, νmax and ΔΠ1 from XP spectra (R ≈ 15–85) "with accuracies comparable to" LAMOST-based (R ≈ 1800) results. They were applied to more than 2.5 million red giants in Gaia DR3. Saliency maps show distinct RGB vs RC spectral behaviour. — [Barman et al. 2026 (arXiv:2604.17045)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17045)
- **Gaia XP → age.** SIDRA-XP, trained on BINGO (APOGEE→APOKASC-2) ages using XP-derived stellar parameters, has residuals of about 0.064 dex for stars near 10 Gyr and was applied to 2,218,154 stars. SIDRA-RVS (RVS spectra) reaches about 0.12 dex. The models are restricted to RGB and high-mass RC stars. — [Almannaei et al. 2024 (arXiv:2412.09040)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09040)
- A transformer foundation model on XP spectra, trained against the APOGEE DR17 DistMass catalogue, jointly predicts Teff, log g, [M/H], mass and age, with spectral inpainting. — [Zhang et al. 2026 (arXiv:2603.03732)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03732)
- Photometric mass map (TESS νmax plus Gaia/colour radii): the first all-sky Gaia–asteroseismology mass map. — [Hon et al. 2021 (arXiv:2108.01241)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01241)

### Inferences
- [C/N] ages are about 2–4× less precise than seismic ages, but they work for luminous giants, where scaling relations fail, and they are insensitive to mass loss (birth mass). Seismology and [C/N] are therefore complementary labels rather than competing ones.
- XP-based age and mass predictions very likely exploit the log g/Teff/[M/H] combination (luminosity-class information) together with broadband CN/molecular band shapes, but I found no explicit XP [C/N] calibration. XP mass/age inherits the label source (BINGO/APOKASC-2 or DistMass) and its domain (RGB + high-mass RC, disc metallicities).
- Validity range of [C/N] clocks: post-first-dredge-up and pre-bump (no thermohaline mixing), roughly 1–10 Gyr. At low metallicity extra mixing starts earlier and birth [C/N] varies, so the globular-cluster extension (Spoo 2025) is essential but sparsely sampled.

### Gaps
- No source found measuring [C/N] (or [N/Fe]) directly from Gaia XP, or from broadband photometry such as narrow-band CN filters.
- Li-based age proxies for giants were not found beyond Cao & Pinsonneault's suggestion of Li as a first-dredge-up/RGB-bump diagnostic.
- Quantitative calibration coefficients and scatter for Casali 2019 and Spoo 2022/2025 were truncated in the abstracts; their metallicity validity limits are not quantified here.

## 3. Data-driven giant ages (Martig 2016, Ness 2016, Mackereth 2019, Leung 2023 and 2023–2026 networks): what they inherit from APOKASC; out-of-distribution behaviour

### Takeaway
Data-driven giant ages now reach about 10–20% precision (Leung 2023 VED: 22% overall, 11% for RC; Anders 2023 XGBoost: 17% median statistical; CatBoost: 20.8%). All of them inherit the seismic age scale (mostly APOKASC-2, increasingly APOKASC-3, APO-K2 and TESS) together with its model choice, mass-loss prescription and metal-poor domain gaps. A 2026 test of 352k MWM DR19 stars shows ages are insensitive to architecture and hyperparameters but sensitive to the training set, with the oldest, coolest and most metal-poor stars most affected.

### Cited Findings
- Mackereth et al. 2019 **[older]**: a Bayesian neural network trained on seismic ages, applied to 65,719 APOGEE DR14 × Gaia DR2 stars (age–velocity-dispersion study). — [Mackereth et al. 2019 (arXiv:1901.04502)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.04502)
- BINGO (Ciucă et al. 2021): a Bayesian NN trained on APOKASC-2 seismic ages with APOGEE. 17,305 stars with BINGO ages were used. The training set was built "to minimise bias", and BINGO is applied only to populations similar to the training set. It is limited to RGB and high-mass RC stars, and it cannot reliably constrain low-metallicity ages because such stars are rare in training. — [Ciucă et al. 2021 (arXiv:2003.03316)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03316); the training restriction and low-[Fe/H] caveat are quoted in [Almannaei et al. 2024 (arXiv:2412.09040)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09040)
- Leung et al. 2023: a variational encoder-decoder trained on paired APOGEE–Kepler stars removes abundance information from the latent space. It needs only about 1,000 precise seismic ages and gives about 22% overall and about 11% for RC. The ages do not depend on [α/M]. Public catalogue: APOGEE DR17. — [Leung et al. 2023 (arXiv:2302.05479)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05479)
- Anders et al. 2023: XGBoost trained on 3,060 RGB/RC stars with Kepler seismic ages, giving 178,825 APOGEE giants with a median statistical uncertainty of 17%. — [Anders et al. 2023 (arXiv:2304.08276)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08276)
- Stone-Martinez et al. 2023/24 (DistMass): simple NNs on Teff, log g, [M/H], [C/Fe] and [N/Fe]. Masses are trained on seismic masses (giants) and isochrone masses (dwarfs), with a mass standard deviation of ±12%. Ages come from the mass–age relation. — [Stone-Martinez et al. (arXiv:2311.17887)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17887)
- Boulet 2024: CatBoost on Teff, L, [CI/N], [Mg/Ce], [α/Fe], U_LSR and Z, trained on TESS SCVZ + APOKASC-2 to reduce data shift and validated on K2 GAP. Median fractional age error is 20.8%: 7–23% for stars older than 3 Gyr, 26–28% for 1–3 Gyr and 43% below 1 Gyr. Applied to 125,445 DR17 stars. Using kinematics as a feature risks circularity for Galactic studies. — [Boulet 2024 (arXiv:2403.20291)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20291)
- Tayar et al. 2026 vary NN hyperparameters, architecture and training set (APOKASC-2, APOKASC-3, APOGEE-K2, TESS) on 351,995 MWM DR19 stars. Ages are "generally insensitive to the neural network hyperparameters or the machine learning architecture, but are somewhat sensitive to the training set". Ages of "the oldest, coolest, and lowest metallicity stars" are most sensitive, and "even simple neural network models are sufficient". — [Tayar et al. 2026 (arXiv:2606.24383)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24383); training-set comparison per [WebSearch summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24383)
- XP and RVS transfer: SIDRA (Almannaei 2024) for 2.2M stars, an XP foundation model (Zhang 2026) and XP→seismic parameters (Barman 2026); details in Q2. — [arXiv:2412.09040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09040); [arXiv:2603.03732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03732); [arXiv:2604.17045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17045)
- Theodoridis et al. 2025 (TESS 132k ages) list Gaia XP spectral coefficients among the features used (per the WebSearch summary). — [arXiv:2505.08109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08109)
- Willett et al. 2026 state their catalogue is meant "to improve training sets for machine-learning-based age estimations", in particular by extending to K2 fields across the Galaxy and to ex situ stars. — [arXiv:2602.06870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06870)
- Systematics inherited from training labels: mass-transfer products (about 3–4% of seismic samples; [Roberts 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10800), [Frazer 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26927)) are age-label outliers that cannot be predicted from spectra. Abundance-based chemical-clock models implicitly assume a Galactic age–abundance relation, which may not hold out of the training volume.

### Inferences
- Effective label noise for a data-driven giant-age model trained on APOKASC-3 is at least 11% random plus about 10% model systematics, plus an extra 3–4% catastrophic outliers from mass transfer. Training-set choice (APOKASC-2 vs -3 vs TESS) dominates over architecture, so label provenance should be an explicit conditioning or nuisance variable.
- Out of distribution means [Fe/H] < −1, the oldest ages (Universe-limit pile-up from prior truncation; Willett flags stars within 1σ of 20 Gyr), luminous giants (log g ≲ 1.5, beyond the seismic calibration), and young or massive RC stars (core overshoot systematics). Models that rely on abundance correlations, as earlier Cannon/[α/Fe]-driven ones did, will impose disc chemistry–age trends on halo and accreted stars. Leung's abundance-decorrelated latent is designed to mitigate this.
- XP-based age catalogues are second-generation transfers (XP ← APOGEE ages ← seismic ages), so errors compound and the domain shrinks to the intersection of the training sets.

### Gaps
- Quantitative OOD metrics (for example accuracy vs [Fe/H] below −1) for Leung 2023, Anders 2023 and the XP models were not retrieved.
- No source here tested whether NN ages for stars in globular clusters or eclipsing binaries reproduce independent ages.
- A 2025–26 astroNN/Leung follow-up on XP ages (if any) was not found in this search.

## 4. Expected future seismic samples (PLATO, Roman, Kepler/K2/TESS reanalyses) and sizes

### Takeaway
Current seismic giant samples are about 15.8k (APOKASC-3), about 19k (K2 GAP), 7.7k (APO-K2) and about 158k TESS detections, with about 133k TESS ages at about 23%. Homogeneous multi-mission reanalyses now reach about 17k PARAM ages (Willett 2026) and 19k in the TESS CVZ. XP-predicted seismic parameters cover about 2.5M giants. PLATO aims at about 10% ages for large giant samples, and Roman's bulge survey could add bulge RC seismology if photometric precision reaches about 1 mmag per 15 min.

### Cited Findings
- More than 9,100 additional Kepler giants without APOGEE spectra (García et al., in prep.) have seismic data, 6,663 of them detected by five or more pipelines. — [APOKASC-3 full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- TESS: at least 158,000 oscillating giants (158,505 QLP detections), about 132,794 with ages at 23%. In the CVZ, 19,151 giants over sectors 1–87, with an 80% increase in known oscillators at T > 8. — [Hon 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01241); [Theodoridis 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08109); [Sreenivas 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00498)
- Multi-mission homogeneous ages: more than 17,000 giants (Kepler + K2 + TESS, PARAM). — [Willett et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06870)
- XP-predicted Δν, νmax and ΔΠ1 for more than 2.5 million bright red giants. — [Barman et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17045)
- PLATO is ESA's M3 mission for transits and asteroseismology of a large number of stars, with Earth–Sun age accuracy of about 10%. — [Rauer et al. 2024/25 (arXiv:2406.05447)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05447). "PLATO as it is" argues that solar-like giants are evolutionary clocks, and Willett 2026 cites PLATO as enabling "estimates with 10% precision for large samples of RGs". — [Miglio et al. 2017 (arXiv:1706.03778)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03778); [Willett et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06870)
- PLATO is also expected to add new clusters with seismic red giants, filling sparse age–metallicity calibration space. — [Tayar & Joyce 2025 (arXiv:2502.09582)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09582)
- Roman Galactic Bulge Time-Domain Survey: cadence and duration suit red-giant seismology. A precision of ≤1 mmag per 15 min for saturated stars is needed to detect the bulge red clump. — [Huber et al. 2023 (arXiv:2307.03237)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03237). Pointing at bulge globular clusters would give cluster seismic ages and integrated mass loss at low and high metallicity — [Molnár et al. 2023 (arXiv:2306.12459)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12459).

### Inferences
- For training data-driven models over the next 2–3 years, the realistic seismic label pool is about 30–40k high-quality giants with spectroscopy (APOKASC-3 + APO-K2 + TESS CVZ + Willett) plus about 130k lower-precision TESS ages. PLATO will not add large numbers before about 2028–2030.
- Bulge and metal-poor coverage remains the main hole. Roman (bulge) and globular-cluster pointings are the only planned remedies, and they are at proposal or white-paper level.

### Gaps
- Predicted numbers of PLATO red giants with seismic ages, and Roman bulge red-giant yields, were not obtained (abstracts truncated or not stated). PLATO's launch date (ESA plans about end-2026) was not verified in this session.
- No information was retrieved on Kepler full-sample reanalyses beyond García et al. in prep., or on Chinese missions (ET/Earth 2.0) for giant seismology.

## 5. Connecting giant age scales to dwarf/subgiant scales (clusters with both, eclipsing binaries) and the size of the cross-regime systematic

### Takeaway
Direct ties exist through a handful of clusters (M67: subgiants through RC with K2; NGC 6791 and NGC 6819 with eclipsing binaries at the turnoff) and long-period red-giant eclipsing binaries. Typical cross-regime systematics are about 2–4% in radius (Gaia: giants +1.7%, dwarfs −2.1%, relative 4%) and about 10% in age (isochrone model-to-model; APOKASC-3 GARSTEC vs YREC). Some clusters (NGC 6819, M4) and metal-poor globular clusters show offsets beyond these levels, so a single global offset is not adequate.

### Cited Findings
- Zinn et al. 2019 **[older]**: relative error between dwarf and giant scaling radii of 4% ± 0.6% at 0.8–30 R⊙ (giants +1.7%, dwarfs −2.1% relative to Gaia DR2 parallaxes, with a 2% systematic floor). — [arXiv:1910.00719](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.00719)
- M67 with Kepler/K2 is the first cluster seismic study spanning subgiants to core-He-burning giants. It provides an empirical surface term, the phase term ε, and calibrated mass/radius scaling relations with quantified systematics. — [Reyes et al. 2025 (arXiv:2502.18714)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18714). 27 M67 stars show small/large-separation deviations tracing deepening convective envelopes from subgiants to giants — [Reyes et al. 2025 (arXiv:2504.01828)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01828).
- Cluster ages adopted in the seismic-vs-isochrone meta-analysis: M67 3.95 Gyr, NGC 6791 8.3 Gyr (from eclipsing binaries near the turnoff), NGC 6819 2.54 Gyr. Seismic medians agree for M67 and NGC 6791 but not for NGC 6819 or M4. The seismic calibrators "are not particularly well-matched to the distribution of galactic ages and metallicities inferred from other methods (e.g. isochrone fitting of subgiants, Xiang & Rix 2022)". — [Tayar & Joyce 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09582)
- The NGC 6791 seismic mass agrees with the fundamental (eclipsing-binary) mass: 1.15 ± 0.01 vs 1.15 ± 0.02 M⊙. NGC 6819 is about 2σ high (1.65 vs 1.55 ± 0.04 M⊙). — [APOKASC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- Red-giant eclipsing binaries give dynamical mass and radius independent of the Sun-calibrated scaling. Ages of 3–5 Gyr have been derived from binary modelling (Brogaard 2018). Coeval solutions were found for 8 of 11 systems (Yıldız 2026), and jointly modelled binaries in which both stars oscillate fix He to 3% (Thomsen 2026). — [arXiv:1801.08167](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08167); [arXiv:2608.18250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18250); [arXiv:2610.36209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36209)
- Isochrone-based cluster ages carry at least about 6% absolute uncertainty (M92 analogue: 5.4%) and about 10% model-to-model scatter. — [Tayar & Joyce 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09582)
- Grid-to-grid age differences on the RGB can exceed 80% when mass is unconstrained, with significant disagreement at non-solar [Fe/H]. — [Morales et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17600)
- APOKASC-3 thick-disc age: 9.14 ± 0.05 ± 0.9 Gyr from lower-RGB stars. — [APOKASC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00102)
- APO-K2 II ages agree with APOKASC-3 to about 3% (a giant-to-giant tie across Galactic fields), and the paper also discusses offsets relative to spectroscopic ages. — [Warfield et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16250)

### Inferences
- At solar metallicity and 2–9 Gyr, giant and dwarf/subgiant scales are consistent at about the 10% level (M67 and NGC 6791 agree; NGC 6819 is off by roughly 2σ in mass, i.e. about 15–20% in age). At low metallicity and old ages, discrepancies are larger and of uncertain sign (M4 offset, the Helmi-stream underestimates from global seismology, Teff-scale issues).
- The seismic thick-disc age (about 9.1 Gyr) is likely younger than subgiant-isochrone thick-disc ages from Gaia-era studies (often quoted at about 11–13 Gyr). This possible cross-regime offset of order 1–3 Gyr for old populations should be checked directly. I did not retrieve the subgiant number in this session.
- A joint dwarf + giant age model should treat the inter-regime offset as a free nuisance parameter, roughly 4% in radius and about 10% in age, possibly depending on [Fe/H] and log g. Clusters and eclipsing binaries are the anchors, but they are sparse at [Fe/H] < −0.5 and at ages above 9 Gyr.

### Gaps
- No direct quantitative comparison retrieved of seismic-giant vs subgiant-isochrone (e.g. Xiang & Rix 2022) ages for the same Galactic populations.
- No 2024–2026 paper found that fits a single model jointly to dwarf/subgiant and giant seismic samples to measure an inter-regime age offset.
- The magnitude of the "offsets between our ages and spectroscopic ages" in APO-K2 II was not retrieved (abstract truncated).
